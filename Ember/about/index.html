About Ember

A small tool that does one job and stays out of your way.

Why it exists

Getting a video out of a social post usually means one of two extremes: an enormous all-purpose downloader, or an online service you have to trust and hope stays up. Neither sits well inside a bot, an app, or a script that only needs to know where the file actually is.

Ember is the middle path – a compact library you can drop into your own project, which finds the media and then gets out of the way. Think of it as a smaller, simpler take on cobalt.

How it works

When you open a post, the page itself quietly asks the site where the video lives. Ember asks the very same question, reads the answer, and passes it on to you together with the title, author and preview image.

Every site is handled by its own small piece of code, so adding a new one does not disturb anything else. When a site serves video in many small chunks, Ember collects them – several at a time if you like – and puts them back together into a file you can play.

What Ember does not do

  • It is not an online service. There is no server of ours in the middle, no account and no tracking. Your computer talks to the site directly.
  • It does not unlock anything you cannot already see. When Ember borrows the login from your browser, it simply acts as you, with exactly the access your own account has.
  • It is not a paywall bypass. Content you have not paid for stays out of reach.

Using it responsibly

Ember is meant for media you are allowed to have: your own uploads, content you hold the rights to, or material you are saving for offline viewing and archiving. Please respect the rules of the platforms you use and the people who made what you download.

Videos and other media on social platforms may be protected by copyright and subject to DMCA and similar laws. The authors of Ember do not endorse, and are not responsible for, the use of this software in ways that violate those laws. Whatever you download, and whatever you do with it afterwards, is your responsibility.

The project

Free and open source under the MIT licence, written in Python and maintained by smeshidojoe. Ideas, bug reports and pull requests are welcome on GitHub – especially support for new sites.

Ember also powers EmberBot, a Telegram bot built on the same library. Other projects live on the main site.
