About Ember

A small extractor that does one job and stays out of your way.

Why it exists

Pulling a video out of a social post usually means one of two extremes: a giant general-purpose downloader you shell out to, or a hosted service you have to trust and keep reachable. Neither fits well inside a bot, a desktop app, or a script that just needs the URL of the file.

Ember is the middle path — a compact library that speaks the sites' own internal APIs, hands back typed objects, and leaves the decision of what to do next to you. It is a deliberately smaller cousin of cobalt, whose extraction approaches it follows.

How it works

Ember calls the same endpoints a site's own frontend calls in the browser, pulls direct media links and metadata out of the response, and packs them into a Result. Each service lives in its own module under ember/services/, so support is added by writing a file rather than by touching the core.

HLS manifests go through a built-in parser: segments are fetched — optionally in parallel — and assembled into a file. With ffmpeg present they are remuxed to .mp4; without it a single stream still lands as a playable file.

What it is not

  • Not a yt-dlp replacement. yt-dlp covers thousands of sites and edge cases. Ember covers eighteen social platforms well and embeds cleanly. Many projects run both.
  • Not a YouTube downloader. Out of scope on purpose.
  • Not a service. There is no backend, no API key, no telemetry. Requests go from your machine to the platform, and nowhere else.
  • Not a way around paywalls. Cookies let Ember act as you, with the access your own account already has — nothing more.

Using it responsibly

Ember is a tool for getting at media you are allowed to have: your own uploads, content you have rights to, material for archival or offline viewing. Respect the terms of the platforms you pull from and the rights of the people who made what you download. What you do with the files is on you.

Project
