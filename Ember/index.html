Python 3.9+ · one dependency

Media extraction that burns clean.

Ember is a small Python library and command line tool. Give it a link to a post – it finds the video, photo or track behind it and hands you a direct link, the details about it, and the file itself if you want it.

No heavy toolchain, no browser running in the background, no online service to depend on. Everything happens on your machine, in a few hundred kilobytes of code.

quickstart.py 
import ember

result = ember.extract("https://vimeo.com/76979871")
print(result.title, result.author)

# direct links, ready to hand anywhere
for m in result.media:
    print(m.kind, m.quality, m.url)

# …or let Ember save the file
paths = ember.download(
    result, "downloads/",
    max_height=720,
    concurrency=6,
)

01 – What you get

Small tool, serious reach

Ember asks a site for media the same way the site's own page does, then gives you the result in a form you can actually use.

🔗

A link or the file – your call

Ember can simply tell you where the media lives, or download it for you. Take the link and do your own thing with it, or let Ember save the file. Both work.

📺

Handles streamed video

A lot of sites serve video in small pieces instead of one file. Ember collects the pieces on its own and puts them back together into something you can play.

🧩

Built to live inside your project

Quiet, predictable and easy to plug into a bot, an app or a script. It stays out of the way and never prints anything you did not ask for.

🍪

Can use your own login

Ember can borrow the session from your browser, so anything you are allowed to see while logged in stays reachable – private posts and age-gated pages included.

🎚️

You pick the quality

See which resolutions a video comes in, cap it at the one you want, and check how many megabytes it will take before anything starts downloading.

📚

Whole sets at once

Grab an entire playlist, or the latest posts from someone's profile, and go through them one by one instead of copying links by hand.

02 – Two ways in

Library or command line

The same engine either way. Install once and you can use Ember from your own Python code or straight from a terminal.

progress.py 
import ember

def on_progress(p: ember.DownloadProgress):
    if p.fraction is not None:
        print(f"{p.fraction*100:.0f}%")

result = ember.extract(url, cookies_from_browser="firefox")

ember.download(
    result, "downloads/",
    concurrency=6,
    on_progress=on_progress,
    embed_metadata=True,
)
terminal 
# show direct links and details – nothing is downloaded
ember "https://x.com/user/status/123456789"

# -d turns downloading on
ember -d "https://vimeo.com/76979871"

# cap quality, 6 pieces at a time, custom folder
ember -d -p downloads --max-height 720 --concurrency 6 "<url>"

# audio only, with the track details written into the file
ember -d --audio-only --embed-metadata "<soundcloud url>"

# private content, using the login from your browser
ember -d --cookies-from-browser firefox "<url>"

05 – Questions

Frequently asked

What makes Ember worth using?

It is small and it is yours. Ember installs in one line, adds almost nothing to your project, and runs entirely on your own machine – no account, no online service, no waiting in a queue. You get the media links along with the title, author and preview, so you can build your own interface around them instead of parsing someone's output. And when a site changes, only one small piece needs fixing.

Do I need to install anything else?

Usually not. Most downloads work with Ember alone. A separate helper called ffmpeg is only needed for a few extras: pulling out just the audio, writing the title and author into the file, and joining video with a separate sound track.

Why does Instagram only give me a low-quality preview?

Instagram hides the good version from anyone who is not signed in. Using cookies will help you to bypass this and get the full media. The same trick works for adult posts on X, closed groups on VK, and most video on Facebook.

Some sites give me nothing at all.

A few of them – Reddit, Newgrounds and OK.ru – refuse requests coming from VPNs and data centres. From a normal home connection they work fine, and you can also route Ember through a proxy.

Which browsers can it borrow my login from?

Firefox works everywhere. On Windows, Chromium-based browsers such as Vivaldi and Opera work too. Recent versions of Chrome, Edge and Brave lock their data away, so those usually cannot be read.

Can I use it in my own bot or app?

That is exactly what it is built for. Ember is free and open source under the MIT licence, so you can ship it inside anything you make. The docs show how to wire it up.
